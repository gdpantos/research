## Research Overview

Our main interest lies in **supramolecular chemistry**. We are trying to understand **how** and **why** molecules interact with each other, and **what rules** govern self-assembly in nature. By answering these questions, we aim to produce organic materials with new properties that may ultimately lead to applications that improve everyday life.

We regularly use microwave-assisted synthesis, HPLC, 1D and 2D NMR techniques, mass spectrometry, isothermal calorimetry, UV-vis, IR Fluorescence spectroscopies, as well as circular dichroism spectroscopy (CD) and circular polarised luminescence (CPL) to investigate chirality.


## Organic Materials

We investigate the properties of naphthalenediimides in conjunction with poly-substituted aromatic groups for electro-optical applications. This work is conducted in collaboration with Prof. Gunther Hennrich (Universidad Autónoma de Madrid).

We also collaborate with Prof. Alain Nogaret on flexible electronic materials based on supramolecular functionalisation of graphitic substrates with polyaromatic derivatives.

Additional collaborations include:

- Dr Adelina Ilie: hybrid organic-inorganic 2D materials
- Prof. Ventsislav Valev: chiral molecules and new approaches for detecting chirality

## Dynamic Combinatorial Chemistry

In this project, Nature is allowed to select the optimal structure, topology, receptor, or ligand from a pool of potentially active molecules using reversible covalent chemistry.

The principal exchange processes studied include:

- Disulfide exchange
- Imine exchange
- Hydrazone exchange

One of our most exciting findings is that structural divergence within racemic mixtures can occur spontaneously in aqueous systems under disulfide exchange conditions. This suggests supramolecular interactions may have contributed to the emergence of biological homochirality.

We are also interested in topologically complex molecular architectures, including knots and interlocked structures.

## Ligands for DNA

We study ligands for biologically relevant DNA structures, including:

- G-quadruplexes
- Double-stranded DNA targets

Our approach combines:

- Molecular modelling
- Organic synthesis
- Advanced analytical techniques

These tools enable us to understand and optimise molecular recognition processes involving nucleic acid targets.
